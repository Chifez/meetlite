# Scheduling and meeting UX benchmarks

**Date:** 2026-10-28

**Purpose:** First-party benchmark evidence for MeetLite’s meeting-card actions, scheduling, invitations, and cross-time-zone behaviour. This is product research, not an assertion of current MeetLite capability.

## What leading products make explicit

| Concern | Google Calendar / Meet | Microsoft Outlook / Teams | Zoom | Product implication for MeetLite |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cross-time-zone invite | Calendar converts created events to UTC and renders them in each invitee’s local zone; an organizer can choose an event zone. Its scheduling UI also shows a shared guest’s current-zone time. [Google time zones](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/37064?hl=en-7), [guests](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/37161?ctx=topic&hl=en&topic=1672471) | Calendar items are saved in UTC and each recipient sees local time; Scheduling Assistant reveals differing attendee zones and adjusted free/busy. [Outlook time zones](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/outlook/getstarted/manage-time-zone-settings-in-outlook), [Scheduling Assistant](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/outlook/sharing/new-outlook-tips-for-executive-admins-and-delegates) | Zoom defaults to the device/profile zone but allows an explicit zone on each scheduled meeting. [Zoom time zones](https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0063717) | Persist the instant **and** organizer-selected IANA zone; display viewer-local time plus the organizer zone where ambiguity matters. Never use a browser-formatted date string as the scheduling value.
| DST and recurrence | Google warns that future/past events can be displayed incorrectly when timezone rules change, though it adjusts timestamps as date changes. [Google DST](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/37064?hl=en-7) | Outlook documents missing and duplicated hours at DST transitions, and says an event crossing a transition has its real elapsed duration. [Outlook DST](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/outlook/daylight-saving-time-in-new-outlook-calendar) | Zoom’s timezone-change support guidance requires host review/updates for affected meetings. [Zoom timezone guidance](https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0089478) | Use IANA zone rules and UTC instants, retain the original zone on recurring series, test skipped/duplicated local hours, and flag affected future occurrences when timezone rules change.
| Invitation delivery and RSVP | Any email address may be invited; non-Google users receive email. Email provides Yes/No/Maybe; organizers can send event updates and track RSVP state. Reminder delivery differs by recipient/calendar service. [Google invitations](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/37161?ctx=topic&hl=en&topic=1672471), [Google RSVPs](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/16713792?hl=en-7) | Teams supports Accept, Decline, Tentative and Follow; organizers receive RSVP emails. Its Outlook flow adds Teams join details when the invite is sent. [Teams calendar RSVP](https://support.microsoft.com/en-gb/teams/meetings/manage-your-calendar-in-microsoft-teams?nochrome=true), [Teams from Outlook](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/teams/meetings/schedule-a-microsoft-teams-meeting-from-outlook) | Automatic invite email is tied to Calendar/Contacts integration; Zoom’s scheduler sends invitations on save when that integration is enabled. [Zoom scheduling](https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0060700) | An email alone is insufficient: issue RFC 5545/iCalendar invitations and sequenced updates/cancellations, record delivery/bounce status, maintain RSVP state, and make recipient-local reminders a calendar responsibility. Send explicit product reminders only as a separate, consented channel.
| Before-start CTA | Meet access is an explicit per-meeting access policy; a host-first setting exists, while defaults vary by product tier. [Google Meet access](https://support.google.com/meet/answer/9302870?hl=en-AS) | Teams lets invitees reach a pre-join screen up to 15 minutes early; access can still route to a lobby. [Teams pre-join](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/teams/meetings/join-a-meeting-without-an-account-in-microsoft-teams) | Default behaviour requires the host; optional join-before-host can be bounded to 5, 10, or 15 minutes, but Waiting Room can override it. [Zoom join-before-host](https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0060501), [waiting room conflict](https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0082131) | Model and explain a join window and access policy. Before it opens, show time/countdown and a disabled or “available in…” CTA; once open, route to pre-join/lobby or room according to policy. Do not infer access purely from clock time.
| Start, leave, and end | Meet access policy determines whether host presence is required. [Google Meet access](https://support.google.com/meet/answer/9302870?hl=en-AS) | Organizers can leave personally or choose **End meeting** for everyone. [Teams end meeting](https://support.microsoft.com/en-au/teams/meetings/end-a-meeting-for-everyone-in-attendance-in-microsoft-teams) | Zoom distinguishes leave/continue (with a new host) from **End Meeting for All**. [Zoom ending](https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0065164) | Separate `start`, `join`, `leave`, and `end-for-all` permissions and state transitions. An organizer’s “Start” should communicate what it changes (e.g., lobby opens/host controls active); destructive end-for-all needs confirmation and a clear terminal state.

## UX recommendations derived from the benchmark

1. In card lists, show one primary action that reflects the person’s role and current access state: **Start** for an eligible host before an active session, **Join** for an eligible attendee during its allowed window, and **Ended**/recap after termination. Avoid showing actions that the server will reject.
2. Pair actions with status, not just timestamps: “Starts in 12 min”, “Waiting for host”, “In lobby”, “Live for 23 min”, or “Ended by organizer”. The server must be the authority for capability and room lifecycle; the client clock only improves anticipation.
3. Make international scheduling visible at creation and invitation review: explicit event zone, local equivalent for the creator, invitee-zone availability where known, and a DST warning for a problematic local time. Render the email/ICS `DTSTART`/`DTEND` as zoned values so recipient calendar clients localize them.
4. Send invite, update, and cancellation messages deliberately, rather than silently mutating a meeting. Preserve a recipient-level audit trail for delivery and RSVP, including non-account invitees.
5. Let organizers choose policy rather than baking one global rule: host-required versus early entry, early-entry length, lobby/bypass conditions, who can end for all, and whether ending stops new joins only or disconnects everyone.

## Source policy

All claims above link to official Google, Microsoft, or Zoom support documentation. Product availability and defaults are plan/admin-policy dependent and should be rechecked during implementation.
